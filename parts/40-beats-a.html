How a beat works

A beat is a short loop of 16 boxes. Each box is a sound or a silence. That's all a beat is.

Tap your foot and count "ONE and-a-and, TWO and-a-and, THREE and-a-and, FOUR and-a-and." The four big numbers are where you'd clap. 4 claps × 4 boxes = 16 boxes = one loop.

A "boom" on every clap: the heartbeat of dance music.

Drummers use three jobs

  • B BOOM (the kick drum): a low thump, on 1 and 3.
  • S SNAP (the snare): a middle "tak," on 2 and 4, where people clap.
  • t tick (the hi-hat): a tiny high "ts" in the gaps.
  • rest: silence. Gaps make the groove.

The FM-1 trick

The FM-1 plays one sound at a time, so we make one short drum-like sound and play it at three heights: a low key = BOOM, a middle key = SNAP, a high key = tick. A funky little robot drummer. Tempo: 70–90 chill, 100–115 pop, 120–128 dance.

Make a drum sound

Turn a preset into a short "hit." You do this once, save it, and use it for every beat in this book.

  1. Turn Presets to 127 PERCUSSION (or 123 TOM TOMS, 106 LOG DRUM, 099 BLOCK). Tap a key: you want a sound that stops on its own.
  2. ENV: Attack lowest, Decay a little, Sustain lowest, Release lowest. Close it. every tap is a short "tok"
  3. LFO: PMD 0, AMD 0. Close it.
  4. FX: turn everything off except Distortion on with Gain a little. Close it.
  5. Press Oct− once. Tap the keys below. a low BOOM, a middle SNAP, a high tick
  6. Press Save. Write the preset number in your log as MY DRUM.
Not drummy enough?

BOOM too thin: press Oct− once more. SNAP too polite: Edit → top key GLO → Feedback up (fizz). Still like a xylophone: try another preset, or a few clicks of Algorithm. Hunting is part of the fun.
