Running SLOOP? Four sounds at once

SLOOP is free replacement software for the FM-1, made by the musician 3dSam. It turns the box into a 4-track groovebox: three synth tracks and a drum track, all playing together. That's the "yes" to page .

  • Try it first, no install: the clickable SLOOP tutorial at fm1-demo.pingywon.workers.dev/sloop/ — six lessons and four ready-made grooves.
  • The big change: tap a button for its pages, hold it and the keys change job. ALGORITHM picks the track. Track 4's bottom keys are 16 drums.
  • Your stock sounds are replaced by SLOOP's own (153 sounds, 37 drum kits), and the lessons in this book describe the stock software.

Putting it on (a computer with Chrome or Edge)

  1. Open isod89.github.io/sloop-fm1. Plug the FM-1 in with a USB-C data cable, straight into the computer, no hub.
  2. Press INSTALL, allow MIDI access, wait for Done. the FM-1 restarts on the SLOOP logo
  3. Unplug and plug the FM-1 back in once.

Getting back

  • Stuck at power-on? Hold Oct− while switching on: SLOOP USB RESCUE.
  • Back to the stock software: on the same installer page, "Return to the official firmware (V15)", with the official FM-1 file from m-vave.com. Back up your SLOOP music first: the stock software can't open it.
Fine print

SLOOP is free, open source (GPL-3.0) and still in beta: its authors say to use it at your own risk. Not made by M-VAVE, and this book isn't made by the SLOOP authors.
